The models aren't wordy. They're compressed.

The usual complaint about AI prose is padding. Here it's the other way round. Most model essays contain no filler phrases at all, where Paul Graham averages about one per thousand words. The models repeat the previous sentence less, and Codex packs far more content words into each sentence. (The brief did discourage hedging and throat-clearing, so the filler gap is partly steered; the rest wasn't asked for.) Clipped, dense prose is its own tell.

One number that sorts them

Take every feature where all three models lean the same way, and ask of each essay: is it nearer the models' rate or Paul Graham's? The share of features where it's nearer the models is its machine-likeness. Every model essay scores 0.61 or more; every Paul Graham essay 0.39 or less. These are the essays the reference was built from, so the gap here is a best case. On a different author's published posts, which the reference has never seen, it still put them below 0.5 and posts an assistant had drafted above 0.7. It measures how sentences are built, so it can't be fooled by sprinkling in someone's favourite words.

How this was measured, and what to doubt

  1. Data. 50 Paul Graham essays (700–3,500 words) and 25 essays each from three models given one detailed style brief and a shared topic list. Endnotes, "Thanks to" paragraphs and [n] markers are stripped; curly apostrophes are normalised.
  2. Features. 40 grammar structures from a WordNet-backed classifier plus 16 surface habits, each measured as a share of sentences. Differences are in units of Paul Graham's essay-to-essay standard deviation.
  3. Steered features. The brief gave explicit rules for dashes, I/you, sentence length and "and" joins, so those measure obedience, not style. They are shown separately and left out of the map and the classifiers.
  4. Uniformity. PG's essays span about 20 years; the models wrote all theirs at once from one topic list. Some of his spread is time and topic, so read the gap as an upper bound.
  5. Scope. One writer, one brief, 25 essays a model. This shows how these models missed this writer, not how to detect AI text in general.
